Consultation & Assessment

History
  • HPI, PMHx, Meds, Allergies, SocHx, FamHx
  • Substance use history: goal (abstinence, reduction, no change); first, daily and last use; amount (current, max) or $ spent; route (IV, snort, p.o., chew, smoke); pattern (binge, daily, PM vs throughout day); complications; abstinence (last period, longest period); previous treatment
  • DSM-5 criteria for substance use disorder
  • ID/OD risk factors (see Harm reduction below)

Substances to ask about: opioids, alcohol, cannabis, stimulants (amphetamines, cocaine), benzodiazepines, nicotine, hallucinogens (LSD, psilocybin, MDMA, ketamine), GHB, inhalants, barbiturates/other sedatives, OTC medications (diphenhydramine, dimenhydrinate, DXM)

Physical exam
  • Intoxication/withdrawal signs: pupils; skin (diaphoresis, piloerection, track marks, abscess); tremor
  • Complications: murmur (infective endocarditis); enlarged liver (chronic liver disease); skin ulcerations (cocaine adulterants / methamphetamine parasitosis)
Investigations
  • UDS, ECG (QTc)
  • CBC, Mg, Na, K, Cl, CO2, glucose, BUN, creatinine, GGT, AST, ALT, ALP, total bili, INR, albumin
  • Daily Na, K, Cl, CO2, glucose, BUN, creatinine (alcohol withdrawal)
  • Chronic EtOH signs: ↑ MCV, ↓ platelets, ↑ GGT, ↓ Mg, AST:ALT ratio >2
  • Blood cultures, ECHO if suspected sepsis/endocarditis
  • Offer STI screening (VDRL, urine GC/CT, HIV, HCV, HBV)
Harm reduction (ID/OD risk factors)
  • Naloxone kit
  • Use with others / at safe injection sites
  • Recent ODs
  • Test dose
  • EtOH swabs before injecting
  • Sharing/reusing needles

Urine drug screen (UDS)

Immunoassay ("urine dip")

  • Cheap, quick, available; false positives and false negatives occur
  • Opiates: morphine and codeine reliably detected; semi-synthetics (bup/nal, hydromorphone, oxycodone) less reliably; synthetics (methadone, fentanyl) not detected
  • Heroin (6-monoacetylmorphine) metabolizes into morphine; morphine metabolizes into hydromorphone (not the other way around)
  • Clonazepam is not detected on the benzo screen
  • Most substances detected within 2–3 days; cannabis may be positive for weeks or months
  • No false-positive cocaine

Opioids & OAT

Pain & OUD: opioids are often needed whether or not the patient is on OAT. Tolerance usually means higher, more frequent doses. Avoid the patient’s opioid of choice if possible. Hold all opioid orders if sedated or RR <10. Cancel outpatient OAT Rx on admission.
Pain management
  • Always offer OAT (buprenorphine, methadone or SROM). It relieves withdrawal, which reduces pain, and patients are less likely to leave AMA.
  • Refuses OAT: mild-moderate pain: acetaminophen-oxycodone in divided doses (max 10 tabs/day). Severe: HM 4–6 mg p.o. q3h ± HM Contin 6 mg bid, or oral morphine 20–30 mg q3h ± MS Contin 30 mg bid; titrate to effect. Very severe: PCA, involve pain service.
  • On OAT: maintain dose and consider additional prn or fixed dosing (may temporarily ↑ OAT). Split dose for analgesia: methadone → BID; buprenorphine → TID. Can reduce buprenorphine to 8–12 mg and add a full agonist (e.g. HM) for acute pain.
  • Follow-up with RAAM or addiction MD after discharge; tight/daily dispensing of opioids at discharge.
Dose equivalents: hydromorphone 2 mg = oxycodone 5 mg = morphine 10 mg = codeine 66 mg. Methadone : SROM = 1:4–8 (dose dependent).
Precipitated withdrawal
Buprenorphine displaces a full agonist if given too early, causing sudden, severe withdrawal. Fix: give more BUP, or adjuncts (clonidine, ibuprofen, acetaminophen, loperamide, dimenhydrinate), or short-acting opioids (e.g. HM).

No missed doses for 3 consecutive days before a dose increase. RFs: age 60+, lung/liver disease, meds that interact with and ↑ methadone.

Missed doses: 1–3 missed: no change. 4 missed: ↓ 50% or to 30 mg (whichever is higher), then ↑ 10 mg/day × 3 days, not above the original dose. ≥5 missed: ↓ to 30 mg or less (re-start), ↑ 10–15 mg q3–5 days; SROM (max 200 mg) can be added at restart if no recent opioid abstinence (e.g. incarceration).

Switch to SROM: convert 1:4 (methadone 60 = SROM 240), ↑ q2d (likely closer to 1:6–1:8). Methadone taper: Day 2 ↓40%, Day 3 ↓60%, Day 4 ↓80%, Day 5 restart.

Carries: 8 weeks on methadone AND illicit opioid use stopped; UDS with visits (more often initially); increase by 1 day/week monthly if stable.

Buprenorphine/naloxone: induction, microinduction, missed doses

Traditional induction

  1. Start once COWS >12 (12 h after short-acting opioids, 24 h after long-acting, ~5 days after methadone)
  2. First dose 2–4 mg (8 mg if COWS >24)
  3. Reassess after 60 min: COWS worse = precipitated withdrawal; withdrawal resolved = no more doses day 1; improved but ongoing = additional 4 mg q1–3h to max 12–24 mg day 1 (higher if inpatient/monitored)
  4. Day 2: give total day 1 dose as consolidated AM dose; reassess at 60 min; max 16 mg (higher if inpatient)
  5. Adjust by 2–4 mg/day. Max 24 mg per monograph; may go to 32 mg

Long-acting injectable SC buprenorphine (Sublocade)
  • Monthly injection, q26–42 days; can start after 7 days of SL buprenorphine (8–24 mg)
  • Loading 300 mg SC monthly × 2; maintenance 100 mg monthly, or continue 300 mg if IV fentanyl/ongoing opioid use
  • Peak 24 h post-injection; covered on ODB
SROM (Kadian) details
  • Start 30–100 mg; 100–200 mg if heavy fentanyl user; ↑ q2d
  • Average 200–800 mg/day (60–1200 in literature)
  • Carries: DWI indefinitely unless high clinical stability or DWI is a barrier; monitor to prevent misuse

Alcohol

Must-ask: last drink (anticipated course), history of seizures/DTs (load vs CIWA only), cirrhosis (diazepam vs lorazepam).
Alcohol withdrawal can be life threatening. Identify at-risk patients and offer aggressive management. Kindling: severity increases with each untreated episode.
Withdrawal timeline (hours since last drink; O = onset, D = duration)
AutonomicO 6–8 h, D 1–2 d

Peak on day 2–3.

Withdrawal management
  • Benzos first line: diazepam, unless liver dysfunction, age >65 or resp depression risk, then lorazepam
  • CIWA (symptom-triggered): not validated in complicated withdrawal history; e.g. diazepam 10–20 mg if CIWA >20
  • Loading (Hx seizure/DTs): diazepam 20 mg q1h × 3 or lorazepam 2 mg q1h × 4
  • Fixed (outpatient, can’t monitor, no complicated Hx): diazepam 10 mg qid → tid → bid → od → stop
  • Phenobarbital: add if non-responsive to high-dose benzos, or DTs
  • Adjuncts (unreliable against DTs/seizures): gabapentin 1200 mg/d (mild-moderate; may continue as anti-craving); pregabalin (anti-craving); carbamazepine 800 mg/d, taper 5–9 d (moderate-severe); valproate 900–1200 mg/d (moderate-severe); clonidine/beta blockers (autonomic hyperactivity)
  • Dehydration is common; consider fluids if tachycardic/non-responsive to benzo
Seizures and DTs
  • Seizure: full work-up (EEG, LP, careful neuro exam) if first seizure, esp. focal signs; may omit if known WD seizure Hx. ¼ have a 2nd seizure: aggressive benzo tx. May progress to DTs (12–24 h post-ictal clearing first): monitor 24 h.
  • DTs: marked tachycardia, tremor, diaphoresis; global confusion, disorientation, impaired attention, sleep-wake disturbance. Aggressive benzo loading (hundreds of mg diazepam equivalents); IV if non-responsive to oral; highly agitated: haloperidol 1–5 mg IM + lorazepam 1–5 mg.
  • Refractory DTs: IV diazepam slowly q5min to light sedation; 2 doses, then 10 mg × 2 q5min, then 20 mg q5min; monitored setting (resp depression).
Psychiatric patients and antipsychotics
  • CIWA false positives: items on anxiety, agitation, hallucinations. Psychosis, severe anxiety/mood disorders risk over-medication; rely mainly on tremor and sweating.
  • Withdrawal prolongs QT (resolves with treatment; worse severity = longer). Sudden death in DTs from Torsades; older antipsychotics for DTs ↑ mortality.
  • ECG (QT) for anyone on antipsychotics in withdrawal; withdrawal may ↑ psychotic symptoms; benzo-load aggressively and use antipsychotics judiciously.
Thiamine and alcohol-related brain damage
Can affect grey and white matter and may be reversible. Give thiamine promptly in ED or AWS (before glucose).
  • Wernicke-Korsakoff: 200–500 mg tid–bid IV × 3–5 d, then 250–300 mg p.o. daily
  • High deficiency risk (admitted): 250–300 mg IV × 3–5 d, then 250–300 mg p.o. daily
  • Low risk (outpatient): 250–500 mg PO × 3–5 d, then 100–250 mg p.o. daily

Wernicke: ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion. Korsakoff: amnesia, confabulation, apathy, lack of insight.

Stimulants, Cannabis, Nicotine, Benzodiazepines

Stimulants (crystal meth, cocaine/crack)
  • Complications: psychosis, agitation, seizure, CVA, MI, arrhythmia, infection
  • Assessment: local infection sites, comprehensive bloodwork, urinalysis + broad-spectrum UDS, ECG, STBBI (HIV, hepatitis, TB, STIs), consider blood cultures/ECHO
  • Withdrawal: depression, dysphoria, psychomotor slowing, excessive tiredness/sleepiness, ↑ appetite
  • Non-pharm: most evidence for contingency management ± CBT; harm reduction counselling
  • Meds (limited evidence): mixed evidence for replacement (LA stimulants, once daily observed, e.g. Vyvanse 20–40 mg to start); modafinil 100–300 mg/d (cocaine); bupropion 150–300 mg/d; topiramate (cocaine, off-label); mirtazapine 15–30 mg qhs (CM in chemsex); naltrexone 50–150 mg
Cannabis (THC)
  • Complications: psychosis, mood/anxiety disorders, memory/cognitive impairment
  • Withdrawal: onset ~12 h, peak ~48 h, duration ~7 days; restlessness, anxiety, dysphoria, irritability, insomnia, anorexia, tremor, hyperreflexia, autonomic hyperactivity
  • Meds (symptom-based): gabapentinoids 1200 mg/d; CBD oil 400–800 mg daily; Sativex titrated (studies: 19 sprays/d); nabilone 0.5–1 mg bid–tid (studies 6–8 mg/d); NAC 1200 mg BID for abstinence maintenance; trazodone, mirtazapine, quetiapine qhs for insomnia. Non-pharm: CBT, MI.
  • Harm reduction: lower-THC products, balance CBD; avoid if personal/family Hx psychosis, pregnant/breastfeeding, age <16; avoid synthetic cannabinoids and dabbing; avoid combustion (vape/edibles); edibles ≤2.5 mg THC, up to 4 h to act, last 12–24 h; avoid deep inhalation/breath-holding; use occasionally (max 1 day/week); no driving for 6 h
Nicotine
  • NRT patch: 1 cigarette = 1 mg nicotine; pack = 20–25 cigs; so 1 ppd = 21 mg. Continue × 6 wk, then ↓ 7 mg q2 weeks. Short-acting: gum, lozenge, inhaler, spray. S/E: skin irritation, nightmares (remove patch at night).
  • Varenicline (Champix): 0.5 mg od × 3 d; 0.5 mg bid × 4 d; 1 mg bid × 12 wk; stop smoking 7 days in. S/E: vivid dreams, insomnia, nausea; marginal ↑ CVD if heart disease; neuropsychiatric S/E disproven.
  • Bupropion (Zyban): NDRI; 150 mg od × 3 d, then 150 mg bid × 12 wk. S/E: seizures, headache, anticholinergic, mood changes.
  • E-cigarettes (harm reduction): Health Canada–approved device; avoid grazing; smoke at discrete times; reduce amount and nicotine concentration; don’t start if you don’t smoke.
